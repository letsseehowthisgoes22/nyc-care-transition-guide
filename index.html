Private outpatient care after psychiatric discharge in NYC

When an adult leaves psychiatric inpatient care, the next week can involve a hospital team, a pharmacy and a new outpatient office. Start with the kind of follow-up the discharge team recommends, then confirm who has accepted the referral, when the first visit is and whom the patient can reach before it.

Set up care around where the patient will actually stay

Use the first-week address, even if that means the patient is temporarily staying in another borough. Compare the whole round trip at the time they would travel, including transfers, accessibility and how they will get home after a late visit. Ask whether the first assessment has to be in person before counting on virtual follow-up.

For a day program, request the first week’s attendance requirements. For a psychiatry practice, ask whether the scheduled visit is an evaluation, a prescribing appointment or an administrative consultation. Confirm whether the office understands the reason for the referral and has the records it needs.

Keep the discharge plan and the outpatient intake in sync

Use the handoff worksheet to track appointment details, records receipt and the interim contact. The patient can decide who they want involved in those logistics and ask staff how to document any permissions. The comparison guide helps distinguish a hospital day program, young-adult outpatient programming and a prescriber's office before making calls.

If the next program gives you an intake date but still has to decide on admission, record the date as an intake. Ask the current team what follow-up it wants in place if the new program cannot begin on the expected date.
