Psychiatric discharge and outpatient follow-up in New York City

Use this guide when an adult family member is preparing to leave a New York City psychiatric inpatient unit or Comprehensive Psychiatric Emergency Program (CPEP). It gives the patient and their chosen supporters specific questions for the hospital discharge team, the next provider and the people helping at home.

Where to start

Ask the discharge team for the first confirmed outpatient appointment, the receiving provider’s direct number and the plan for prescriptions, records and questions until that appointment. If a visit is still pending, record which person at the hospital will follow up and when.

Ask for the confirmed appointment before leaving

New York’s February 2026 joint guidance directs covered psychiatric inpatient programs and CPEPs to schedule and confirm psychiatric aftercare with an identified provider within seven calendar days of discharge. It also describes what those units must do when they cannot secure a timely appointment. Ask your discharge team how the guidance applies to your unit and what has been arranged. [1]

A useful take-home plan gives you a name, date, time, full address or video instructions and a number that reaches the receiving office. Ask the hospital team to include what it has shared with the next provider, how the patient can access the discharge instructions and whom to call before follow-up if a question arises.

  • AppointmentAsk whether the receiving office has booked the time and checked that it can see the patient. Confirm whether the visit is intake only and what comes after it.
  • PrescriptionsReview the team’s written instructions with a clinician before leaving. Ask which pharmacy has the prescriptions and who handles questions or refill problems before the first visit.
  • Consent and contactsTell the team who the patient wants involved. Ask how to document permissions and use the hospital’s or provider’s secure channel to handle records.

Find a workable outpatient option in the right borough

If the team needs more options, NYC Mental Health Connect can help you look for mental health and substance use services by factors such as age, language, insurance and availability. Use the address where the person will actually stay after discharge, especially if that puts them in a different borough. Ask the discharge team to check suitability and send any necessary referral securely. [2]

Directly confirm what the receiving office accepts, including new patients, the insurance plan and the requested service. If the insurer denies coverage or you face an unexpected out-of-pocket cost, ask for the reason in writing. New York State’s CHAMP helpline, 888-614-5400, helps people navigate mental health or substance use coverage problems. [3]

  • Call to verifyAsk about the earliest booked appointment, the exact clinic location, transit and accessibility needs, fees and whether the office needs a referral or authorization.
  • Keep one decision openIf the appointment is delayed or canceled, record who will call the discharge team and by what time. Ask that team about interim support and the next available option.

Agree on who does what through the first appointment

The person leaving the hospital should be able to see the plan and choose what help to ask for. Put the next appointment, travel plan and contact numbers in one place they can reach. Give each agreed task a person and a due time. After the first visit, ask the patient what practical support they want next.

Example handoff entry: the outpatient clinic confirmed Tuesday at 10 a.m. Jordan will arrange a ride after the patient confirms they want one. The patient will contact intake if the clinic’s appointment confirmation has not arrived by Monday at noon.

  • If something changesKeep the discharge team’s contact handy. Ask the treating team which number to call after hours and follow its written instructions.
  • Urgent helpCall 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency. For mental health or substance use crisis support and local referrals, call or text 988. [3]

Common questions

Do I need to find my own outpatient provider after psychiatric discharge?

Start with your hospital discharge team. New York guidance tells covered inpatient programs and CPEPs to confirm an aftercare appointment with an identified provider within seven calendar days. Ask your team how it is handling the referral and what it will do if timely care is unavailable. [1]

What if my appointment is only a walk-in referral?

Read the applicable section of the state guidance with the discharge team. For covered psychiatric inpatient programs and CPEPs, the guidance says a walk-in referral alone is insufficient. Ask whom to call, what follow-up the team has arranged and whether the next appointment is confirmed. [1]

How can family members help an adult after discharge?

Ask the patient what support they want. Rides, appointment reminders and help picking up prescriptions are practical options. The patient can discuss with the hospital what information it may share with specific people and how to record that preference.

Where can New Yorkers get help with denied mental health coverage?

Ask the insurer for its explanation and retain the plan’s reference number or letter. New York’s CHAMP program can help with questions about access, denials or unexpected charges involving mental health or substance use care. Call 888-614-5400. [3]
